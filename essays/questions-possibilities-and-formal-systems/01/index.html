      • From Conviction to Inquiry

      2 minStefan Kober

      Conviction does not fill orientation evenly.

      Some things are settled enough that we simply rely on them.

      The floor will hold. This is the road home. That person is a friend.

      Seven plus five is twelve.

      The meeting begins at nine.

      Other parts remain uncertain, incomplete, unstable, or contested.

      Why did she say that? Will the project succeed?

      Which explanation fits what happened? Was the measurement wrong?

      What should we do if the attack comes through another route?

      Sometimes something that had seemed settled becomes uncertain again. A new fact no longer fits. An expected result fails to appear. Someone asks a question we cannot answer. An alternative we had not considered suddenly becomes visible.

      Inquiry begins somewhere inside this mixture.

      It does not begin from nothing. Most of orientation remains in place while some part becomes uncertain, tense, or newly visible.

      Something that had remained in the background becomes an object of attention.

      A gap or tension becomes difficult to ignore. Something matters that we do not yet know how to settle.

      We cannot simply choose what will convince us. But we can ask questions, search for alternatives, compare possibilities, preserve them outside memory, and enter procedures designed to constrain what happens next.

      These activities do not determine the result of inquiry.

      They change its conditions.

      How far can such intervention go?
